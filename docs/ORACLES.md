# Oracle protocols

Oracle responses are external premises supplied under an explicit agreement. A valid response does not make a subjective judgment mathematically true. The former normalized oracle-count score was removed in 0.4.

## Roles and agreement

A protocol declares observation, classification, legal assessment, or institutional determination. It names providers and versions, fixes the question and evidence digest, and describes the procedure. A single-provider agreement accepts that provider's assertion. Unanimity requires every named provider to respond with the same polarity.

A missing response is pending. An abstention contributes no premise. Opposite responses under unanimity are disputed and cannot supply an oracle step. A response can be withdrawn through a timestamped event; the earlier recording cutoff remains replayable.

## Confidence has a declared meaning

Confidence is optional. If used in a response or a threshold, the protocol must explain what it means. Passing a numerical threshold does not establish calibration, truth, legal burdens, or agreement between providers. No confidence value is invented for the Long pilot.

## Assurance instead of a score

Every report separates checked derivation, legal fidelity, corpus completeness, evidence authenticity, oracle authentication, counterargument search, and judicial acceptance. Zero oracle responses does not establish any of the other dimensions: interpretation assumptions and judicial findings are still premises.

## Inspect the actual pending request

Open the Argument workbench and choose Pending subjective assessment. The Long context removes the supplied gross-disproportionality finding and asks for an agreed legal assessment. The placeholder provider has not agreed or responded. The live Rust engine reports pending evidence.

Provider names, versions, and hashes are checked for consistency. Cryptographic signatures and external identities are not authenticated by this prototype. See the [current specification](SPEC-0.4.md).
